1. Overview

Banega answers "aaj khaane mein kya hai?" for a whole household. It stores every family
member's food rules once, then suggests recipes for each meal that the most people can
eat. Suggestions come from an AI assistant called Maharaj.

Problem. Indian homes decide three meals a day across mixed diets (Jain, veg, egg, non-
veg), spice levels and health needs. The decision usually happens between the person
cooking (family, cook or house help) and whoever is home, from memory.

Goals for v1

    Onboard a household and every member's preferences in under 3 minutes.
    Return 3­6 meal options per query, each labelled with who can eat it and what to
    tweak.
    Turn chosen dishes into a daily cooking ticket for the cook, with required items
    checked against kitchen stock.

Reference prototype. The clickable design on the Banega canvas (artboard "App
prototype") is the source of truth for layout, copy and motion. Its recipe list and AI replies
are hard-coded; this document defines the real behaviour behind them.

5.9 Out-of-stock handling

When a required item is Out, Maharaj tries to save the same dish first, and only then
suggests a different one. The family always approves a dish change.

1. Trigger. The cook marks an item Out on a ticket, or the stock check at ticket creation
    finds one.

2. Same dish, different recipe. Maharaj searches for an alternate recipe of the same dish
    that avoids the missing item, using only In-stock items (e.g. no dahi for kadhi  kadhi
    with buttermilk from milk + lemon). The result must still pass the matching engine for
    everyone who could eat the original.

3. Substitute ingredient. If no alternate recipe exists, suggest a safe swap (e.g. tomato
    puree for fresh tomatoes) with the change in quantity.

4. Different dish. If neither works, suggest up to 3 replacement dishes for the same slot
    that are fully in stock and rank highest for the family.

5. Approval. Steps 2­3 can be accepted by the cook alone if the admin allowed it in
    settings; step 4 always goes to the admin, who picks a dish or lets the cook choose.

6. Timeout. If nobody answers within 30 min of the due time, the cook may use the top
    suggestion; the ticket records who approved what.

6.3 Recipe tags the engine needs

Every recipe must carry: diet (veg / egg / nonveg), jain_safe (bool), spice (1­3), meal slots
(nashta / lunch / dinner), cuisine, cook time in minutes, and tags from this set: low_oil,
low_sugar, protein, kid, light, rainy, special, maida. Tagging is done by the content team
with AI assistance and human review.

7. Maharaj AI assistant

Maharaj is an LLM that turns the user's words into structured filters, lets the matching
engine pick recipes, then writes a short Hinglish reply. The LLM never invents recipes,
ingredients or nutrition claims.

7.1 Request pipeline

1. Parse. Send the query, the last 6 chat turns, the household profile and the current slot
    to the LLM. It returns JSON only: {slot, filters[], cuisines[], member_focus,
    max_minutes, keywords[]} .

2. Retrieve. Query the recipe database with those filters (hard filters first, then keyword
    and embedding search). If fewer than 3 results, drop soft filters one at a time and note
    which were dropped.

3. Rank. Run the matching engine (section 6) on the results; take the top 4.
4. Write. The LLM writes one message of at most 2 sentences plus 2­4 follow-up chips,

    using only the ranked results and their fit data.
5. Return the message, recipe IDs, fit summaries and chips to the app.

7.3 Reply rules

    Hinglish by default; switch to the language of the query when it is clearly English or
    Hindi.
    The first line states the count and the filters applied (e.g. "Dinner ke liye 4 options --
    halka").
    The second line names how many options work for the whole family and the key
    constraints respected.
    No medical advice. "Low sugar" and similar are preference tags, not health claims.

7.4 Recipe sourcing

    v1 ships with at least 1,000 everyday home recipes covering every region of India, each
    with photo, tags, ingredients mapped to staples, and steps.
    Recipes are gathered from publicly available sources. The content team rewrites steps
    in Banega's own words and format, uses photos that are shot, generated or licensed,
    and stores and credits the source link on Recipe detail.
    Do not scrape sites whose terms forbid it. The "Video recipe" link must point to the
    recipe's own source.

13. Decisions, assumptions and open questions

Assumptions made in this document

    Native mobile app first; no web app in v1.
    Cooking is a role: most often mom or another family member, sometimes a hired cook,
    often both. WhatsApp is only a fallback for a hired cook without a smartphone.
    Recipes come from a curated, licensed database, not live web scraping.
    Meal-slot cut-off times (11:00 and 16:00) are defaults and may need tuning.
